Probate, inherited homes, and cash buyers, by the numbers

Wealth-transfer totals, inherited-home volumes, senior home equity, probate timelines and costs, and investor and cash-buyer shares, from published sources.

Three markets meet in a probate buyout. One is the transfer of wealth itself, measured in trillions. One is the court process that governs it, measured in months and in a percentage of the estate. One is the housing market that absorbs the houses, where cash buyers and small investors set the pace for inherited homes. This page keeps those three series separate and labeled, because a wealth-transfer forecast, a probate cost range, and a cash-buyer share are not interchangeable — and blending them would overstate what any one of them proves.

What the figures show together is a market that is large, slow, and cash-heavy: a record pool of senior home equity becoming inherited houses, a court process that takes the better part of a year at best, and a buyer pool in which cash and small investors already dominate the lower-priced segment where inherited homes cluster.

How to read these numbers

Included: a research-firm wealth-transfer forecast; a property-data firm's deed-record count of inherited-home transfers; a senior-equity index reading; executor-guide timeline estimates and two published probate cost ranges (kept separate, never blended); a national realtors' association survey of cash buyers; an investor report's quarterly purchase shares and prices; and land-policy figures on heirs' property and uniform partition law adoption. Excluded: any single national probate case-volume total — see below — and any figure visible only in a secondary compilation without a checkable public source page.

Forecasts are labeled as forecasts wherever they appear. Guide estimates are labeled as estimates. Company- and association-reported figures are shown with their reporting dates so a reader can judge their age.

Where no reliable figure exists: no single published national series for total annual probate case filings was found in a checkable public source during this build, so no probate-volume total is stated on this page rather than estimated. Likewise, no reliable national figure for the share of probate estates that end in a buyout — as opposed to a sale or a keeper arrangement — was found, so none is stated. Both gaps are held open for a future refresh with a sourced figure, not filled with an estimate.
